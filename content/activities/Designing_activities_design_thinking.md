# Designing Playful AI Activities

### A design thinking lens for the sprint

*After IDEO's design thinking process*
*Playful Programming and Algorithmic Intelligence*

---

## You are about to design for humans

Not for a curriculum. Not for a learning objective.

For **a specific class, on a specific Tuesday afternoon, in a specific room.**

> *"The main tenet of design thinking is empathy for the people you're trying to design for."*
> — David Kelley, IDEO

Your students are the people. This hour gives you the moves.

---

## The rhythm underneath everything

IDEO describe the core rhythm as **creating choices and making choices**.

**Creating choices** — go wide. Many bad ideas, fast.
**Making choices** — narrow. Commit to one and build it.

Most failed activity design is a team that **never went wide**, or one that **never committed**.

Watch which one you are doing today.

---

## Seven phases, not a checklist

IDEO compare the phases to ingredients in a recipe: structure to start with, improvisation once you know them.

You will move between them messily. That is correct.

But you should be able to say, at any moment, **which one you are in**.

---

<!-- _class: lead -->

# 1 · Frame the question

---

## Start with "How might we..."

Not: *"I want to teach classification."*
But: **"How might we help students notice that a machine's confidence tells them nothing about its accuracy?"**

The move is to define the challenge before reaching for solutions — which keeps the design space open long enough for a good idea to arrive.

**Your first ten minutes:** write three "How might we" questions. Pick one.

---

<!-- _class: lead -->

# 2 · Gather inspiration

---

## Your students are the research

You already have the field data. Use it deliberately:

- Where did your class get confused last term?
- What do they already believe about AI, wrongly?
- What made them lean forward this year? What made them reach for their phones?

Understanding people and context is what fuels insight.

**Ten minutes.** Write down three real things about the actual class you are designing for.

---

<!-- _class: lead -->

# 3 · Synthesize for action

---

## Find the one idea worth building around

Sort, cluster, and connect what you noticed until themes appear.

Look at your three student observations and your "How might we".

**Name the single concept** your activity will carry. One. Not four.

An activity that teaches one thing well beats one that gestures at everything.

---

<!-- _class: lead -->

# 4 · Generate ideas

---

</style>
## Go wide before you fall in love

Twelve minutes. Aim for **eight rough ideas**, not one good one.

Forcing moves when you stall:
- What if it were entirely unplugged?
- What if students had to *break* it?
- What if the AI played a character?
- What if the wrong answer was the point?

Wide exploration is what expands the range of possible solutions.

Then converge: pick one. Kill the rest without mourning.

---

<!-- _class: lead -->

# 5 · Make ideas tangible

---

## Build the roughest thing that can be tried

Not a lesson plan. A **playable version**.

Paper, sticky notes, a half-working p5 sketch, one slide, a prompt typed into a chat window — whatever makes the idea testable in the next hour.

You learn fastest when an idea takes shape in the world.

**Rough is a feature.** Polished prototypes make testers polite.

---

<!-- _class: lead -->

# 6 · Test to learn

---

## The playtest is the point

Another group plays your activity as your students — including the awkward ones.

Testing is what reveals what works, what does not, and what needs another pass.

Your job while they play: **say nothing, write everything.**

Every place you had to explain something is a place the design failed, not the tester.

---

<!-- _class: lead -->

# 7 · Share the story

---

## And then: October

The final phase is articulating the need, the process, and the value in a way others can act on.

Two audiences:

**Your students**, in the weeks between now and 5 October.
**This room**, when you come back and tell us what actually happened.

The real test is not the playtest. **It is your classroom.**

---

## Today's timebox

**Frame + Gather** → 20 min
**Synthesize** → 10 min
**Generate** → 15 min
**Make tangible** → 45 min
**Test** → 30 min

Go wide first. Commit hard after.
