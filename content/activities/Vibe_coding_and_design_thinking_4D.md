# Designing at the Speed of Vibes

### Where design thinking meets vibe coding, through the 4D AI Fluency Framework

*Playful Programming and Artificial Intelligence · University of Copenhagen*

---

## Three loops, secretly the same loop

IDEO's design process for educators. Vibe coding. The 4D AI Fluency Framework.

Three different vocabularies, invented for three different problems — that turn out to describe the exact same underlying rhythm: **frame it, try it, judge it, own it.**

Once you see the overlap, each one makes the other two sharper.

---

## Recap — IDEO's design process for educators

1. **Discovery** — understand the challenge, gather inspiration
2. **Interpretation** — find the story, frame the real opportunity
3. **Ideation** — generate ideas, then refine them
4. **Experimentation** — build prototypes, get them in front of people
5. **Evolution** — track what you learned, move forward

---

## Recap — the 4D AI Fluency Framework

**Delegation** — deciding whether, when, and how to hand something to AI

**Description** — communicating clearly enough to get something useful back

**Discernment** — judging what comes back

**Diligence** — owning what you did with it, and saying so

---

## Recap — the vibe-coding loop

**Ask** — describe the vibe in plain language
**Run** — press play, immediately
**Look** — does it feel right?
**Adjust** — say what to change, in the same plain words

Repeat until it feels done. That's the whole method.

## How the three line up

| IDEO phase | 4D competency | Vibe-coding move |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Delegation | Deciding this is worth building at all |
| Interpretation | Description | Framing the brief in one plain sentence |
| Ideation | Description, at volume | Trying many vibes, cheaply |
| Experimentation | Discernment | Ask → run → look → adjust, on repeat |
| Evolution | Diligence | Writing down what broke, and why |

---

## Discovery = Delegation

Before any prompt: **should this even be a thing I build?**

What's the real problem — a bored fifteen minutes, a concept nobody can picture, a classroom management headache? Delegation asks that question *before* Ideation gets to run wild.

`word-magnet-standalone` exists because someone decided a fridge-magnet haiku toy was worth building. That decision came first.

---

## Interpretation = Description

A brief that fits in one sentence beats a brief that fits in a paragraph.

*"I want it to sound like a nervous houseplant."* *"I want it to lie to me confidently."* Not a spec — a feeling, sharp enough for the AI to build toward.

This is the same craft as writing a good exam question: role, audience, constraint, one clear ask.

---

## Ideation = Description, at volume

Design thinking says: generate a lot of ideas before you fall in love with one.

Vibe coding makes that nearly free. Fork the sketch five times, try five word banks, five personas, five system prompts — in the time it used to take to sketch *one* idea on paper.

Cheap iteration isn't a shortcut around ideation. It **is** ideation, running at a different clock speed.

---

## Experimentation = Discernment, in real time

A prototype isn't a claim. It's a question you're asking a room.

Every "ask, run, look, adjust" cycle is a live discernment call: *does this actually work, or does it just look finished?* Fluent output and correct output are not the same thing — the whole reason "The Lying, Confident Machine" exists.

---

## Evolution = Diligence

The design-thinking phase everyone skips under time pressure is the one Diligence is built for: **write down what happened, including what didn't work.**

Which prompt produced the fabrication. Which fork you abandoned and why. What you'd tell a colleague picking this up cold. That record is what makes the next iteration — yours or someone else's — faster.

# Why this matters for Day 3

---

## Your design sprint is all five phases, compressed

**Discovery + Interpretation** — what's the classroom problem, in one sentence?
**Ideation** — fork a standalone, try three vibes fast
**Experimentation** — playtest with another table, watch them use it
**Evolution** — revise from their feedback, write down what broke

You're not learning a new process today. You're running one you already know, at AI speed.

## A worked example

**Discovery:** teachers struggle to make "bias" concrete for students.

**Interpretation:** *"I want the model to confidently invent a stranger's biography from nothing."*

**Ideation:** three system-prompt variants — lie detector, vitals monitor, fire alarm.

**Experimentation:** run all three, watch which produces the sharpest classroom moment.

**Evolution:** the fire-alarm frame won — write down why, keep the other two as backups.
