# Coding Without Feeling Like a Coder

### A playful intro to "vibe coding"

*Playful Programming and Algorithmic Intelligence*

---

## You don't need to know code to make something real

Every sketch you've played with this week — the haiku machine, the room-narrator, the liar — started as someone typing what they wanted in plain English.

Not syntax. Not semicolons. Just: *"I want this."*

---

## Think remix, not blank page

Nobody writes a song by inventing music theory first.

You take a beat you like, change the lyrics, slow it down, see how it feels.

Vibe coding is the same move, aimed at a little program instead of a song.

---

## Step 1 — Grab something that already works

Don't start empty. Start with a sketch that already runs.

*"Make me one like this, but about dragons instead of robots"* is a completely legitimate way to begin.

---

## Step 2 — Say the vibe out loud

*"I want it to feel sleepy and warm."*
*"I want it to lie to me confidently."*
*"I want it to sound like a nervous houseplant."*

That sentence is the whole instruction. The AI turns it into code — you just have to know what you're going for.

---

## Step 3 — Press play, and look

Run it. Watch what happens on the screen.

Does it feel right? Too fast, too weird, too boring? Say so, in the same plain words.

---

## It's completely fine not to understand every line

You don't need to read the code to know if a haiku is good, or if a webcam is telling you the room is bright.

Your eyes and your gut are the whole test. That's not cheating — that's the job.

---

## Small changes, big surprises

Someone swapped one list of words — *neon, chip, cyborg* — for another — *dragon, castle, giggle*.

Same everything else. Completely different feeling. One tiny edit was the entire idea.

---

## If it breaks, that's just information

A blank camera. An error message. A robot that answers total nonsense.

None of that means you did it wrong. It means: try the next small thing.

---

## Give yourself permission to make something silly

The best way in is not "build something useful." It's "build something that makes you laugh."

Useful comes later, if it wants to.

---

## You don't even need a special tool

A plain chat window — the same one you already use — does this too.

Type what you want. It builds something you can see and click on, right there in the chat. Look at it, say what to change, repeat. Same loop, zero setup.

---

<!-- _class: lead -->

## Try it Now - Just do it!

Open a chat window and describe something small and silly. Or pick a sketch you liked this week, copy it, change one sentence.

Press play. See what happens. That's it — that's the whole craft.
